A LITTLE ABOUT ME

My name is—wait, just look at your search bar.

I just realised that that is more words than just saying my name.

Quick question: is the double “that” correct? Sorry, English hasn’t really been my strong point.

Oh yeah, back to what I was saying: I’m what the average person might call “smart,” “a nerd,” or any ten-letter word there is for it. I don’t really like those labels. And no, no, no—I don’t want anyone calling me a dullard or an ignoramus, either.

Slight side track: you wouldn’t believe how many words there are for negative things to say about a person. I know from experience: I once read through the Merriam-Webster Dictionary for a Scrabble tournament.

THE POINT

Smarts are a resource.

People shouldn’t be sorted into “smart” and “dull.” Sometimes people just need the books, encouragement, and chances to grow what they can do.

A THOUGHT I KEEP COMING BACK TO

A QUICK AGE CHECK

How old am I?

So, like, I was 16 years, 9 months, 5 days, 13 hours, 35 minutes, 5 seconds, 427 milliseconds, 683214 nanoseconds old when I wrote this.

Apparently, I just realised that I’m actually Gen Alpha, not Gen Z. NO!!! I refuse!!! But on a serious note, why did I have to be born in 2010? Why couldn’t I have come out 12 days prior? Nonetheless, I’m Gen Z, and no one is going to tell me otherwise.

I know you’re wondering why I wrote this. You see, late-night funk can affect the brain.

BACK TO THE POINT

People need room to grow.

Those considered dull may just not have the people, books, or conviction to nurture their skills. I’m not saying it isn’t good to be a genius—I’d relive my 16 years of life to be one. But the world needs to treat smarts as a resource, not an object.
